\chapter{Proposed Graph-Based Transformer Model for Depression Detection}
\label{chapter4}

\section{Experimental Results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
The proposed model was built using the PyTorch framework and trained on a CUDA-enabled GPU to
accelerate the learning process. EEG data from the MODMA dataset were divided into fixed-length
windows, yielding 15{,}659 EEG windows with shape \(32 \times 256\) (channels \(\times\) time
samples). The optimized set of 32 EEG channels was kept uniform across all experiments. A
5-fold subject-wise cross-validation strategy was used, where subjects were split into five
non-overlapping folds. In each fold, one subject group was used for testing and the remaining
subjects were used for training and validation. This protocol prevents leakage of EEG windows
from the same subject across train and test sets. Training ran for 20 epochs per fold in a
supervised learning setting. Model optimization used backpropagation with a binary classification
loss, and validation performance was monitored to assess convergence and generalization.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
Model performance was assessed at the subject level by aggregating window-level predictions for
each subject. This aligns with clinical practice where diagnosis is performed at the subject
level rather than on short EEG segments. The evaluation metrics include accuracy, precision,
recall (sensitivity), and specificity to provide a balanced assessment of performance for both
MDD and healthy control (HC) subjects.

\subsection{Class-Wise Performance Analysis}
The model performs consistently across folds for both MDD and HC classes. Precision, recall, and
F1-scores remain high, indicating effective reduction of both false positives and false negatives.
In particular, sensitivity to MDD is high, which is critical for clinical screening. Balanced
class-wise results indicate that the graph-based transformer captures functional connectivity
patterns that distinguish depressive and healthy brain states.

\subsection{Training Dynamics and Stability}
Training and validation loss curves converge within early epochs and stabilize thereafter.
Minor fluctuations are expected due to window-level EEG variability, but validation accuracy
remains high and consistent. The use of functional connectivity as a soft graph bias within the
attention mechanism stabilizes training by highlighting informative inter-channel relationships
without imposing rigid graph constraints.

\subsection{Comparison With Existing Methods}
The proposed framework achieves \(98.61\%\) accuracy on MODMA, outperforming or matching
state-of-the-art EEG-based depression detection approaches, including CNN-based models
(\cite{acharya2018,wang2022}), hybrid spatio-temporal architectures~\cite{zhang2023}, attention-based
transformers~\cite{tigga2022,hou2025}, and recent graph-based methods such as LightK-DSGCN
(\cite{lightk}), GIGN~\cite{gign}, and STGE-Former~\cite{stgeformer2025}. Unlike STGE-Former, which
explicitly combines graph convolutions with transformers, the proposed approach integrates
functional connectivity as a soft inductive bias, avoiding rigid message passing. A comparison
with conventional feature-based methods (e.g., GA-LASSO) is also provided~\cite{movahed2021}.
Table~\ref{tab:comparison} summarizes key results; best values are highlighted in bold.
